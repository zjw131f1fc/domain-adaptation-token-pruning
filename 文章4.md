The training process for the RCP modules is conducted for one epoch using a subset of 10,000 samples from the VQAv2 dataset \cite{goyal2017making}. For benchmarks that provide dedicated training data, we perform targeted adaptation to ensure the model aligns with specific task requirements. We utilize an initial learning rate of $5 \times 10^{-5}$ combined with a cosine learning rate scheduler, where the minimum value reaches 0.1 times the initial rate. We anneal the Gumbel-Sigmoid temperature from 1.5 to 0.2 during training. The balancing of different objectives is achieved by setting the task loss weight to 1.5 and the sparsity loss weight to 200, while the repair loss weight is 3.0. Within the repair objective, we assign a weight of 3.0 to the variance alignment term to stabilize the feature distribution. We set the number of pruning queries to 16. The structural placement of the modules is determined by the specific pruning budget. When the target budget is set to 192 or 128 tokens, we insert the pruners at layers 4 and 14 and 24. For a more aggressive budget of 64 tokens, we shift the pruner locations to layers 1 and 13 and 25. The delayed repair adapters are consistently positioned at layers 22 and 29 to ensure stable feature compensation throughout the generation process.

To evaluate the effectiveness of the RCP framework, we conduct extensive experiments on seven widely used multimodal benchmarks. These datasets include GQA for visual reasoning \cite{hudson2019gqa} as well as MMBench for comprehensive evaluation \cite{liu2024mmbench} and MME for perception and cognition \cite{fu2023mme}. We also include POPE to assess object hallucination \cite{pham2024h} along with ScienceQA for multimodal science questions \cite{saikh2022scienceqa} and VQAv2 for visual question answering \cite{goyal2017making} as well as VizWiz for visual accessibility tasks \cite{gurari2018vizwiz}. We utilize LLaVA-1.5-7B as our primary upper bound model which signifies the full architecture using all visual tokens without any pruning operations. Furthermore we extend our evaluation to the LLaVA-1.5-13B model across the same set of benchmarks to verify the scalability and robustness of our method across different model scales. In our reporting we provide both the absolute task performance on each benchmark and the relative percentage compared to the corresponding upper bound model to support a thorough analysis of the efficiency and accuracy trade off. By adopting these comprehensive evaluation protocols across multiple architectures we demonstrate the capability of our method to maintain high reasoning quality while significantly reducing token redundancy.
