```latex
\subsection{Training and Optimization}
\label{sec:training}
We design the training objective to align the representations of the compressed model with the original full-token model while achieving a specific sparsity target. We keep the backbone parameters frozen, as they are already optimized for multimodal tasks, and update only the pruner and repair modules. The training signal is derived from three primary objectives which address task performance and representation consistency and sequence sparsity.

We compute the alignment loss by matching the statistics of the hidden states specifically in the answer generation region.

\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{repair}} = \lVert \mu_p - \mu_o \rVert_2^2 + \alpha \lVert \sigma_p^2 - \sigma_o^2 \rVert_2^2
\end{equation}

Where $\mu_p$ and $\mu_o$ represent the mean of the hidden states for the pruned and original models along the token dimension, $\sigma_p^2$ and $\sigma_o^2$ denote the corresponding variances, and $\alpha$ is a weighting term for the variance alignment. To enforce the sparsity constraint, we define the global average retention rate across all pruning layers.

\begin{equation}
    r^{(\ell)} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N m_i^{(\ell)}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \bar{r} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{L}_p|} \sum_{\ell \in \mathcal{L}_p} r^{(\ell)}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{sparse}} = \left| \bar{r} - r^* \right|
\end{equation}

Where $r^{(\ell)}$ represents the ratio of kept tokens at layer $\ell$ based on the current binary mask $m$, $N$ is the total number of initial visual tokens, $\bar{r}$ denotes the average retention rate across all pruning layers in $\mathcal{L}_p$, and $r^*$ is the target retention rate. We use an annealing schedule for $r^*$ to ensure a smooth transition from dense to sparse regimes during the early stages of training. Finally, we combine these terms with the standard cross-entropy loss for next-token prediction to form the total loss function.

\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L} = \lambda_{\text{task}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{task}} + \lambda_{\text{repair}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{repair}} + \lambda_{\text{sparse}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{sparse}}
\end{equation}

Where $\mathcal{L}_{\text{task}}$ represents the task-specific prediction loss and $\lambda$ represents the respective weighting coefficients for each objective. With these optimization targets, we guide the model to achieve high sparsity while maintaining representation fidelity relative to the reference model.
```

```latex
\subsection{Training and Optimization}
\label{sec:training_en}

We design the training objective to align the representations of the compressed pruned model with those of the original full-token model while enforcing a target sparsity level. Since the backbone is already well optimized for multimodal tasks, we keep backbone parameters frozen and update only the pruning and repair modules. The training signal consists of three components. It includes a task loss for next-token prediction, a representation alignment loss for repair, and a sparsity regularizer.

We compute the repair loss under \emph{teacher forcing} and restrict the alignment to the answer-generation region, where repair is applied. For a given decoder layer $\ell$, we collect the selected hidden states into a matrix $H \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times D}$, where $T$ is the number of tokens included in the masked region and $D$ is the hidden dimension. We then compute feature-wise statistics over token positions:
\begin{equation}
    \mu(H) = \mathbb{E}[h], \qquad
    v(H) = \mathbb{E}[h \odot h] - \mu(H) \odot \mu(H),
\end{equation}
where the expectation is taken over token positions and $\odot$ denotes element-wise multiplication. Here, $\mu(H)\in\mathbb{R}^{D}$ is the per-dimension mean vector and $v(H)\in\mathbb{R}^{D}$ is the per-dimension second central moment, which corresponds to diagonal variance.

Let $H_p$ denote the student hidden states produced by the pruned model with the repair adapter optionally enabled. Let $H_o$ denote the teacher hidden states produced by the original full-token model with all tokens kept and repair disabled. We align the student and teacher distributions by matching the first and second central moments:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{repair}}
    = \frac{1}{D}\left\lVert \mu(H_p) - \mu(H_o) \right\rVert_2^2
    + \alpha \cdot \frac{1}{D}\left\lVert v(H_p) - v(H_o) \right\rVert_2^2,
\end{equation}
where $\alpha$ balances the contribution of the second-moment term. The $\ell_2$ norms correspond to mean squared error averaged over feature dimensions. Importantly, this objective is permutation-invariant with respect to token order and does not require token-wise correspondence, which is particularly suitable when token pruning changes the token set. In practice, we compute the alignment loss on a set of designated layers and average them to obtain the final $\mathcal{L}_{\text{repair}}$.

To facilitate analysis of pruning-induced representation drift, we additionally report a distributional distance derived from a diagonal-Gaussian approximation. Using the same feature-wise moment estimates, we define
\begin{equation}
    d_{W_2}^2
    = \frac{1}{D}\left\lVert \mu_p - \mu_o \right\rVert_2^2
    + \frac{1}{D}\left\lVert \sqrt{v_p} - \sqrt{v_o} \right\rVert_2^2,
\end{equation}
where $\mu_p=\mu(H_p)$, $\mu_o=\mu(H_o)$, $v_p=v(H_p)$, and $v_o=v(H_o)$. This metric is permutation-invariant with respect to token order and is consistent with our alignment objective in the sense that it vanishes when the first and second central moments match. Although its second term measures mismatch in the standard-deviation space rather than directly in the variance space used in $\mathcal{L}_{\text{repair}}$, it provides a more interpretable scalar for reporting and visualization of drift.

To enforce sparsity, we define the layer-wise retention ratio at pruning layer $\ell$ as
\begin{equation}
    r_{\ell} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N m_{i,\ell},
\end{equation}
and the global average retention rate across all pruning layers $\mathcal{L}_p$ as
\begin{equation}
    \bar{r} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{L}_p|} \sum_{\ell \in \mathcal{L}_p} r_{\ell}.
\end{equation}
We then impose the sparsity loss
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{sparse}} = \left| \bar{r} - r^* \right|,
\end{equation}
where $N$ is the number of initial visual tokens, $m_{i,\ell}$ is the binary keep indicator for token $i$ at layer $\ell$, and $r^*$ is the target retention rate. We anneal $r^*$ during early training to ensure a smooth transition from dense to sparse regimes. Finally, we combine task, repair, and sparsity terms to form the total loss:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L} = \lambda_{\text{task}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{task}} + \lambda_{\text{repair}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{repair}} + \lambda_{\text{sparse}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{sparse}}.
\end{equation}
```
