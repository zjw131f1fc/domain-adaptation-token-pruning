\subsubsection{Pruner Quantity and Placement.}
We investigate the impact of the number and placement of pruning modules within the language model layers. Our results indicate that employing three pruning stages yields higher accuracy than using only one or two stages. This improvement is consistent with the intuition that a multi-stage approach enables a more progressive and adaptive reduction of visual tokens, allowing the model to preserve essential information while gradually discarding redundant features throughout the decoding process.

Regarding placement, we observe that initiating pruning in earlier decoder layers is beneficial. Among the configurations evaluated, the L1+L13+L25 setting achieves the best overall accuracy. This suggests that removing redundant visual information early can help subsequent layers focus on the most relevant multimodal evidence, reducing interference from less informative visual tokens.

\subsubsection{Hyperparameter Sensitivity.}
We evaluate the sensitivity of our framework to the weighting parameters $\lambda_{\text{repair}}$ and $\alpha$ as summarized in Table~\ref{tab:ablation_hyperparameters}. Here $\lambda_{\text{repair}}$ denotes the coefficient of $\mathcal{L}_{\text{repair}}$ in the overall training objective, while $\alpha$ controls the strength of the second-moment alignment term within $\mathcal{L}_{\text{repair}}$. Performance remains competitive across a range of settings, and the best results are obtained when both parameters are set to 3. In contrast, setting either parameter to 0 leads to a clear drop in performance, indicating that both the repair objective and second-moment alignment contribute to maintaining reasoning quality under aggressive pruning.
